Book 2 Source Brief

**Carl's Doomsday Scenario** (Dungeon Crawler Carl book 2) - the Third Floor, the Over City

*Research for Dungeon Crawler World book 2. November 26, 2026.*

This brief collects what book 2 contains, so the book 2 game stays true to the source. It draws on the Dungeon Crawler Carl fan wiki (read through its API, about 60 pages), a 25-chapter summary site and a spoiler recap. Section 9 holds first ideas for putting the game together, including the level-type variety you asked for. It is not the design doc yet. Spoilers from books 3 and later were cut, except two small facts that are flagged where they appear.

2\. Rules for the book 2 game

Agreed in this conversation. These should open the book 2 design doc so other sessions pick them up.

- **One game per book.** Book 2 covers Floor 3 only.

- **Look and feel: late NES as remembered.** A modest step up from book 1, which is the baseline that felt right. Richer chip audio with extra voices and samples, line-scroll and wobble effects, cutscenes between acts, bigger bosses and somewhat more animation. Same NES palette, 320x180 screen and font. Don't overdo it.

- **CRT scanlines stay**, on by default as in book 1.

- **Adult audience.** A private game for you and a few close friends, not for sale. Vulgar language, gore and adult humor are in, because they are in the book.

- **Dressed as in the book.** A character who is topless in the book is topless in the game. It's rare in the book, which is why it lands when it happens. On this floor that's Tsarina Signet (only a thong), Bautista (a shirtless tiger man) and the headless Krasue bodies.

- **Pop culture references** from the early 1980s to the early 2000s are part of the voice. Section 8 lists the ones book 2 makes.

- **Original writing.** Dialog, System AI descriptions and achievement text are written fresh in the book's style, never copied from the novel. Names of characters, items, quests and achievements come from the book.

- **Rule book conflict.** Book 1's DESIGN.md says to keep it PG-13. Book 2's design doc should replace that line with these rules.

3\. How Floor 3 works

The world

- **Arrival:** a golden-brick hallway under a fake starry ceiling, with a red carpet to the game guide's door, where crawlers pick a race and class. Stepping back out opens onto a village. Start locations are random, so crawlers from everywhere are mixed together.

- **Look:** a fake sky with stars and a colorful nebula at night and a red sun by day that bathes the villages in crimson. The ground is wooden slats with smoke drifting up through the gaps. It smells of sulfur and is hotter than Floor 2.

- **Size:** about 300,000 square km, roughly Arizona, mostly ruins.

- **Lore:** the floor is the top layer of a magical world inside a dormant volcano. Long ago, Scolopendra, a monster at the bottom, breathed out the Cataclysm, a nine-way poison attack that killed or mutated 90% of everyone. Survivors' descendants live in the villages and the mutants roam the ruins. The Skyfowl once ruled the whole Over City.

Villages

- Faux-medieval towns with wooden-slat streets, shops, guildhalls and inns. They have no names, only sizes (tiny to extra-large). Killing a mayor lets a crawler name the town.

- **Village Guard Swordsmen**, Level 75, in full plate with long blades, are on duty only in daylight. There is no jail, only the death penalty, for attacking NPCs, robbing shops or passing out drunk in the street. Guards won't follow into the ruins, but a crawler who flees can never return to that village. At night the guards sit invulnerable in their depot, recharged by a soul crystal.

- Killing ordinary citizens gives zero XP.

The ruins

- Moss-covered brick buildings, overgrown parks and fog, full of mobs. Safe rooms and restrooms are scattered through them as on earlier floors.

- Discolored rocks outside village gates raise a hologram signpost listing directions and distances to settlements, safe rooms, restrooms and stairwells. Hidden Dungeon Locator legends sit at major intersections; Carl's Escape Plan skill can read them.

Day and night

- Monsters are always out but worse at night. Villages are safe by day. At night only their main roads are (usually) safe, and the alleys get more dangerous than the ruins. The janitor mobs, Street Urchins, only come out at night.

Safe rooms, stairs and quests

- **Safe rooms:** village inns count as safe rooms. They are styled as Renaissance-fair pubs with the familiar three TV screens and are run by Bopca. Rooms and food now cost gold: the Belly-Rubbed Pug charges 10 gold a night, the One-Eyed Narwhal 20. Bare safe rooms with no food also exist.

- **Mordecai** is Donut's manager on this floor. He teleports into any safe room the party enters and can't fight mobs.

- **Stairwells:** many only appear once a condition is met. The Floor 4 stairs in the circus park appear only after the circus quest. (Book 3 adds that a city boss sitting on a stairwell hides it.)

- **Quests start here:** a new Quests tab, mostly simple tasks. Unfinished quests vanish when you leave the floor. Group and event quests also begin on this floor.

- **Elites:** one-of-a-kind scripted characters from side productions, playing out soap-opera plots for viewers, such as Vengeance of the Daughter. After the Meet an Elite achievement they show on the map as dots with a black cross. An Elite can also be a boss.

- **The show:** views climb from about 372 trillion at the start to 1.9 quadrillion by day 3. Odette's leaderboard draft at the end has Carl 6th and Donut 7th. Sponsorships open on Floor 4, but early bids start here.

7\. Carl, Donut and Mongo's toolkit

Carl

- **Compensated Anarchist:** a monk/rogue hybrid built on traps, bombs and fists, plus a knack for working the cameras. 25% less damage with bladed weapons; damage spells cost 25% more mana. As a Primal, his higher skills can train to level 20.

- **Skills:** Escape Plan (reads hidden map legends, finds stairs), Find Trap, Hide in Shadows, Bomb Surgeon, Backfire, Cesta Punta (jai alai) and Alchemy.

- **Spells:** Fear, Heal, Protective Shell (from the boxers, long cooldown) and Wisp Armor (brief immunity to mind control).

- **Freeballing** (the boxers upgrade): +100% damage on attacks from below the waist. In game terms, kicks and stomps hit twice as hard, which fits book 1's kick moves.

- **Xistera:** a jai alai scoop strapped to his right forearm that launches items from his inventory. He can't punch while it's out. It replaces his slingshot and fires Hob-Lobbers.

- **Explosives:** Carl's Jug O'Boom (his torch-lit Molotov: a blast, then a wide burning area), Hob-Lobbers (smaller than a baseball, fused or impact), goblin and hobgoblin dynamite with remote detonators, and smoke bombs and smoke curtains.

- **Gauntlet:** a small stun chance, and it hurts ghosts.

Donut

- **Spells:** Magic Missile (about level 8 to 9), Clockwork Triplicate (two clockwork copies of a pet or minion for a minute), Second Chance (raises a dead mob to fight for her), Puddle Jumper (short teleport), Torch and Heal. **Acute Ears** shows much more on her minimap.

- **Defenses:** 9 Lives halves the first nine physical hits each day, and she's immune to poison and disease. Her crown adds a chance of sepsis until it crumbles in the finale. Cockroach keeps her alive through a zero-HP hit.

Mongo

- Attacks any mob on sight, bites Donut's nose, will do anything for pet biscuits and screams at the pet carrier. Fang Caps let him hurt ghosts. Grows from dog-sized to pony-sized at Level 13.

Combos from the book

- **Panic:** Carl casts Fear, then Donut fires Magic Missile.

- **The Boomy Boy:** a clockwork Mongo copy carrying dynamite.

- **The Mic Drop:** throw a lit Jug O'Boom straight up, then teleport out with Puddle Jumper.

9\. Putting it together (first ideas)

Book 1 used a TMNT-style overworld with side-scrolling stages behind each entrance, then broke the pattern on Floors 10 and 11 with races and the parade. Book 2 mixes level types the whole way through, the way Contra switched between side-scrolling and into-the-screen base stages. Each type below comes out of a beat in the book, so the variety serves the story, and each should feel the way we remember those games, not the way they play today.

A playable overworld

Your Addams Family idea: the overworld isn't a map you move a token across. It's played. You walk the streets of the Over City from screen to screen, fighting light enemies on the way, and the doors, manholes, tents and gates on those screens lead into levels that each have their own feel.

- **Side-view street screens** linking the starting village, the ruins, the circus park and the Skyfowl settlement, with branches up and down through stairs, ladders and doorways. Light enemies along the way: lemurs on rooftops, Brain Boilers drifting about, and Street Urchins at night.

- **Villages are the same kind of screens,** with shops, inns, the Desperado Club's pub door and NPCs to talk to.

- **The world changes as the story moves:** the circus vanishes, the Floor 4 stairs appear in its park, and the admin building becomes a crater.

- **The 8-day clock drives it,** with day and night. By day, guards keep the villages safe and some doors are shut. By night the guards go home, Street Urchins and Shadow Leaks come out, and alley levels open.

- **Guard law:** attack a citizen in town and the guards come for you. Flee into the ruins and you're banned from that village, as in the book.

- **A map screen on pause** fills in as you go, and signpost rocks and the Dungeon Locator (Carl's Escape Plan skill) reveal more of it.

Other notes

- **Bosses that aren't slugfests,** as in the book: Grimaldi is a set piece, the Divider is a corpse that explodes, and Quill dies to planted dynamite. Heather and the Dispenser are the stand-up fights.

- **New mechanics from the book:** remote charges, the xistera launcher, Krasue two-part kills, darkness that powers up Berserkers, the camouflaged Dispenser found by Donut's ears, Mongo growing mid-game and Katia joining late.

- **Cutscenes** (the late-NES upgrade): class selection, Signet's story, the Danger Zone show, the Remex reveal and Odette's epilogue.

- **Continuity with book 1:** book 1's game hatched Mongo on Floor 3 and made Katia a Floor 5 companion. In the books, Mongo has already hatched before Floor 3, Katia joins late on Floor 3, and Signet is an Elite, not a companion (an early-version leftover in book 1's code, not part of the game). Book 2 follows the books.

- **Saves across books:** book 1 keeps its save in the browser under dcw_save_v1, and book 2 will sit on the same site, so it can read it. If a book 1 save exists, book 2 offers to import it: unlocked skins, a record of book 1 achievements, and a veteran bonus. Levels and stats don't carry, because book 2 starts where the novel's Floor 3 starts. With no save, the game starts from a standard point: Carl, Donut and baby Mongo with their end-of-book-1 gear. Book 2 writes its own save, and each later book reads all the earlier ones.
